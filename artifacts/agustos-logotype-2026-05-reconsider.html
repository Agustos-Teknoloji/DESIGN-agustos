Ağustos Design System · Logotype reconsideration · 2026-05-10

Slick. Modern. Timeless. Three candidates, one decision.

The current logotype is Plus Jakarta Sans Light (v1.7). The brief — modern, timeless, slick, clean — already describes Plus Jakarta. So this comparison tests four hypotheses: maybe a lighter weight gets us to "slick", maybe a Swiss neo-grotesque feels more "timeless" than a humanist sans, maybe a 2026 geometric face feels right, and — newest addition — maybe Inter Tight + Inter as a paired family system replaces the whole serif/sans split. Same lockup mechanics across all faces (lowercase, -0.005em tracking, 1.4× cap-height symbol, 0.4em gap).

Control · Current

Plus Jakarta Sans · 300

Geometric humanist. The v1.7 winner. Warm apertures, double-storey a, humanist ğ.

Control · Lighter

Plus Jakarta Sans · 200

Same face, ExtraLight weight. Tests whether "slick" is really about thinness.

Challenger · Swiss

Hanken Grotesk · 300

Neo-grotesque. More uniform, less humanist warmth. Closer to the Helvetica/Inter axis.

Challenger · Geometric

Geist · 300

Vercel's open font. Modern, geometric, of-this-moment. Risk: tech-coded.

Late add · System

Inter Tight + Inter

Paired sibling system. Inter Tight for logo/headings, Inter for body. Retires Newsreader.

What 200 buys you

Genuinely slicker at 64px. Reads more "couture" — closer to luxury fashion logotypes. The lockup feels more open and confident at hero size.

Why this might win

Closer to the Helvetica/Inter axis without being either. More uniform letter widths, less visible character. Reads as "publisher's house" rather than "designed wordmark" — which fits your philosophy. Aged better than humanist fonts in our experience: the 1957 grotesques still feel current.

Why this might not

You may miss the warmth. Hanken's g is a single-storey loop similar to Plus Jakarta but slightly more mechanical. Risk of feeling generic — the price of neutrality. Latin Extended coverage is solid; ğ reads cleanly.

Why this might win

Most overtly modern of the three. Geometric construction echoes the rotational geometry of the Laz Güneşi — they pair sympathetically. Excellent at small sizes; designed for screen-first contexts.

Why this might not

Tech-coded. Geist reads as Vercel/dev-tool to anyone in that orbit, which is a decent share of the design-aware web. For a publisher whose brands include lighting, archive, and luminaire — that may fight the editorial cream substrate. Verify Turkish ğ by eye in the breve test above.

Why the system play makes sense

Inter and Inter Tight are by the same designer (Rasmus Andersson), share a skeleton, and ship as variable fonts. Tighter proportions in Inter Tight give the logotype a more "locked" feel; Inter at body keeps everything in one harmonic family. Latin Extended including ğ is among the best of any open-source sans. Designed screen-first.

What you'd be giving up

Newsreader. Replacing the editorial serif with Inter for body is the bigger decision here — the system would shift from "publisher's editorial register" to "designed-product register." That may or may not be what you want. Also: Inter is the most-used font on the modern web. Ubiquity is timelessness's twin, but it can also read as default.

Proposed · Inter Tight + Inter

What you keep

One harmonic family from logo to caption. Inter Tight headlines feel locked and confident; Inter body is the most rigorously screen-engineered open-source sans (hinted, kerned, optical-sized). Single foundry, single voice. Excellent Turkish coverage.

What you lose

The serif. Newsreader gives long-form prose a different temperature than any sans — call it editorial gravity. Inter for body reads as product-app, not publisher. For lighting/archive/luminaire content this matters; for SaaS it wouldn't. Also: rule #2 in DESIGN.md ("the publisher precedes the brand") leans on serif body to signal "from Emre's house."

Recommendation · 2026-05-10 · v2 with Inter Tight

The Inter Tight + Inter system is the boldest move on the table. It's also a different decision than the others.

Four of the five candidates are logotype swaps — change one font, ship. Inter Tight + Inter is a system overhaul: it retires Newsreader from body and consolidates the entire identity into one paired family. Those two decisions shouldn't be made on the same scorecard. Treat them separately.

As a logotype face alone — Inter Tight is a strong candidate. Best-in-class Turkish ğ, hinted for screens, paired-skeleton harmony with Inter, and a "tight" axis that genuinely earns the "slick" descriptor without going couture-thin. As a wordmark, it slots in cleanly next to the Laz Güneşi.

As a system replacement — pause. The biggest decision in your DESIGN.md isn't the logotype face; it's that the body is a serif. Newsreader is what makes the system read as a publisher's house rather than a product company. Replace it with Inter and the system shifts register — not necessarily worse, but different. For Pataraz luminaire copy and PLD Türkiye archive content, that shift is consequential. For Photometric Batch UI, it's neutral. For Ağustos Teknoloji proposals, it's downgrading toward generic-modern.

The four-way decision tree:

→ "I want the wordmark to feel slicker, period." Try Plus Jakarta 200 first. Same family, one weight tweak.

→ "I want the wordmark to feel more timeless and disappear." Hanken Grotesk 300. Swiss neo-grotesque, 70-year track record, most "publisher's house" of any candidate.

→ "I want a 2026 face." Geist 300. Risk: tech-coded.

→ "I want one paired family throughout — and I'm willing to retire Newsreader." Inter Tight + Inter. The cleanest, most rigorous system. Cost: editorial gravity.

My honest read on the Inter system: if you're trending toward a product/SaaS aesthetic for Photometric and Pataraz, Inter Tight + Inter is the right move and you should commit. If you're holding the line on publisher / editorial — long-form blog, designer-first proposals, archive depth — Newsreader is doing structural work the system needs, and a logotype swap (Hanken Grotesk or Plus Jakarta 200) gets you the "slick" without giving up the gravity.

Look at the side-by-side editorial preview above. Read it twice. The right answer will be obvious within 30 seconds.
