Sans-only · Five Candidates

One family doing every job — five candidates in the running.

Same locked-down system, rendered fully in sans. Editorial content on the left, product UI on the right. The same family does both jobs in each pairing.

Each candidate uses italic for deck and blockquote — sans italics generally read as "slanted body" rather than a different voice. This is the biggest single trade-off in going sans-only. Pay attention to whether the italic in each face has its own character or feels like a compromise.

Trade-off worth flagging

Sans-only optimizes for Photometric Batch at the cost of Pataraz and PLD Türkiye.

Three of your four brands lean editorial. The premium positioning of Pataraz ("high-end visual at mid pricing") leans on editorial register to feel premium without spending. PLD Türkiye is a publication archive — sans makes a publication look like a tech blog.

If you go sans-only, you're betting that simplicity beats per-brand fit. That's a defensible bet — but it's a bet, not a free upgrade. The system you're seeing below is real and works. It just optimizes differently than what we built before.

Face 01 · The SaaS standard

Inter

Designed by Rasmus Andersson specifically for screen UI. The most-used SaaS sans of the past five years. Tabular numerals, deep OpenType features, true italics. Free, open source.

Reads cleanly at every size. Looks like every premium tech product in 2026 — Stripe, Linear, GitHub, most of YC. Cost: invisibility.

Family: Inter
Weights: 300–800
Mono: JetBrains Mono

Face 02 · Premium tech, humanist

IBM Plex Sans

IBM's open-source corporate face. Slightly humanist, slightly mechanical — designed to balance technical precision with warmth. The "considered" sans choice.

Has a stronger personality than Inter. The italic actually has character (rare for sans). May feel "designed" or "of-the-2010s" depending on taste.

Family: IBM Plex Sans
Weights: 300, 400, 500, 600, 700

Face 03 · The 2026 design-conscious choice

Geist

Vercel's open-source sans, released 2024. Designed to feel "premium European editorial" — what Söhne and GT America offer commercially, but free. Tighter and more confident than Inter.

The most "of-the-moment" choice. Strong identity, especially on display sizes. Risk: it's becoming the new ubiquity for design-conscious tech (Vercel, Linear post-rebrand, several YC companies).

Family: Geist
Weights: 300–800
Mono: JetBrains Mono

Face 05 · The editorial-sans option

Inter Tight

A tightened sibling of Inter, designed for display and editorial use. Same skeletal forms but with reduced spacing and tighter letterforms. The closest sans to Söhne or GT America that's free.

If you want sans but with editorial register, this is the one. Headlines feel confident; body still readable. Shares Inter's UI virtues at small sizes. Best of both worlds.

Family: Inter Tight
Weights: 300–800

Six checks specific to sans

Sans typefaces are easier to confuse with each other than serifs. Run these checks to find the real differences.

1. The italic test

Read the deck and the blockquote. Does the italic feel like a different voice or just slanted body? In sans, this is the hardest thing to get right. Most fail it.

2. The body comfort test

Read the full Turkish body paragraph. Does your eye glide through, or does it tire? Sans body at 16px should feel as comfortable as a serif — most don't.

3. Bold contrast

Find "spesifikasyon disiplini". Does bold pop confidently, or aggressively? Sans bold tends to shout where serif bold announces.

4. The H1 voice

"Işığın mimariyle buluştuğu yer." Read the H1 in each face. Which voice would you want representing Ağustos? Pataraz? PLD?

5. The numerals

"2,847" and "1,240" in the product UI. Tabular, designed for data. Which feels most considered without feeling decorative?

6. The "everywhere" test

If this face appeared on every page of every brand for the next five years — would it feel like your house? Or just like everyone else's?
