Sans + Newsreader · Pairing Comparison

Two faces, one system. Four candidates.

Each pairing renders editorial content (Newsreader, the serif baseline) alongside product UI (the candidate sans). The two halves should read as siblings, not strangers.

All four sans candidates are paired with the same Newsreader serif for the editorial side. The only thing changing is the sans. Watch how each pair feels — do they share a temperament? Does the sans feel like part of the same family, or like it crashed the party?

Pair 02 · The SaaS standard

Inter + Newsreader

Inter is the most-used SaaS sans in 2026. Designed by Rasmus Andersson specifically for screens. Tabular numerals, true italics, deep OpenType features. The Stripe / Vercel / GitHub default.

Risk: Inter is everywhere — your product UI may look like every other SaaS. Reward: nothing reads more cleanly at small sizes in dense layouts.

How to evaluate a pairing

Pairs aren't just about each face individually — they're about whether the two halves feel like siblings or strangers. Six checks, in order.

1. Sibling test

Look at the editorial and the product UI together. Do they feel like two voices in the same publication? Or like two unrelated apps in different tabs?

2. Eyebrow handoff

The eyebrow ("BAYILIK · AYDINLATMA") is in the sans. Does it transition smoothly into the serif H1 below it? Or does it feel grafted on?

3. UI density

The product UI panel — table, stats, buttons — needs to read at small sizes in dense layouts. Which sans feels most "made for this"?

4. Numerals

The "2,847" and "1,240" stats use tabular numerals. Which sans's numbers feel most considered? Sharp without aggression?

5. Citation handoff

The Richard Kelly quote attribution is in the sans. Does it feel like a coda to the serif quote, or a different writer's signature?

6. Long-term feel

If you saw this pairing on every Photometric Batch screen, every Ağustos page, every Pataraz catalog — would it still feel right in two years? Or would it look "of 2026"?
